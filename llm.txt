MATCH landing page
https://match.spaceth.co

This site explains CE-7 MATCH, the Thai particle detector on the Chang’e-7 lunar orbiter. One detector axis has two opposing entrances: space-facing and Moon-facing. Four tracking layers, each with crossed X and Y strips, make eight Silicon planes. The conceptual stack from space toward the Moon is First Silicon, Second Silicon, First ΔE, Central E, Second ΔE, Third Silicon, Fourth Silicon.

Science to keep:
Cosmic rays are energetic particles, mostly protons and nuclei, plus electrons. They are not light.
Solar cosmic rays are solar energetic particles from flares and eruptions.
Galactic cosmic rays are a steady high-energy rain from beyond the solar system, likely from supernova remnants.
Jovian electrons are electrons accelerated in Jupiter’s magnetosphere.
Lunar albedo is secondary particles leaving the surface after an interaction. It is not reflected light.
Earth’s magnetosphere is compressed sunward and stretched into a magnetotail anti-sunward. The Moon’s orbit crosses that tail for several days each month. The diagram is not to scale. MATCH rides with the Moon.
MATCH flies about 200 kilometres above the Moon in a near-polar orbit. The orbit drawing is schematic, not a reconstructed trajectory. The viewing cones are illustrative.

Chang’e-7 has an orbiter, a lander, a rover, and a hopper. The lander is planned to touch down on the illuminated rim of Shackleton crater, near the lunar south pole. MATCH stays on the orbiter. The route animation is schematic and is not a reconstructed flight path. The launch site named in the copy is the Wenchang Spacecraft Launch Site on Hainan.
